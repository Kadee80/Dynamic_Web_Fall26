# Midterm Presentations

**Today. Five minutes each.** Order is shuffled at the start of class so nobody
has to sit and dread being last.

The full brief is [Week07/Midterm.md](../../Week07/Midterm.md) — this page is
just the running order for the day.

## Before you sit down

- **`npm run dev` and `npm run server` already running.** Two terminal tabs,
  started before class, not while thirty people watch.
- **Your app already in the state you want.** A few real items in `db.json`,
  not an empty list and not forty rows of "test test test".
- **Your slides open** if you are using them. Strongly recommended — they keep
  you to time and stop the demo from wandering.

## When it is your turn

In this order, and it is the same order as the brief:

1. **Elevator pitch** — one to three sentences. What the app is, and who for.
2. **Your user story**, and the field it produced.
3. **Your developer checklist** — what you thought it would take.
4. **Your revised checklist** — what it actually took, and what you forgot.
5. **Live demo.** Add something, edit it, delete it, **refresh**. Show it at
   phone size, using responsive mode in dev tools or just a narrow window.
6. **Code walkthrough of the new field** — where it lives in the form, how it
   gets to the server, how it comes back, and how it serves the story.

**If you cannot explain a line, do not show it.** That is not a trick question;
it is the single clearest signal of whether the work is yours.

### The thing most people undersell

**The gap between your two checklists.** Everybody's lists differ, and the
interesting part is *which* thing you forgot — the edit form, the PUT that
dropped a field, the rows already in `db.json` that have no value for it at
all. Say what you missed and what it cost you. Nobody is marked down for
having been surprised by their own code; that is what the exercise was for.

## When it is not your turn

**Laptops closed.** Participation is 25% of your grade and this is one of the
days it is most visible.

You will also see three or four solutions to problems you are stuck on. Every
semester somebody solves in week 9 the exact thing somebody else is fighting
with, and the only way to find that out is to be watching.

## What is graded today, and what is not

| | |
|---|---|
| **Today** | Presenting is what marks the midterm **on time** |
| **Friday, midnight** | Your **code** is graded from whatever is pushed to your repo |

So the days between are still build time. If you present something half-broken
and fix it Wednesday, that counts — tell me in your push, and if the project
changes substantially after today, email me so I know to look again.

If you make a change you are proud of after presenting, it is worth a line in
your `README.md` saying what and why.

## If something goes wrong in your demo

It happens, to everybody, every semester. Say what you think it is, move on,
and come back to it if there is time. **Debugging out loud in front of the room
is a better demonstration of competence than a demo that went smoothly**, as
long as you keep talking.

What you should not do is restart the server four times in silence. If it is
not back in thirty seconds, carry on with the code walkthrough.
